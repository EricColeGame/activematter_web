## Understanding the Active Matter Time Loop: Lore Meets Mechanics

In the fractured world of *Active Matter*, you play as an operative deployed by a shadowy corporate employer into high-risk anomaly zones. These sectors do not obey conventional physics; instead, multiple parallel timelines overlap and collide. The **Active Matter time loop** serves as both the narrative engine and the core mechanical foundation of the game's extraction loop. 

To harvest valuable resources, specialized devices called "time anchors" tether a quantum-unstable zone to our physical reality. However, these anchors cannot hold indefinitely. They merely isolate the territory for a fleeting window, creating a strict raid timer. Once the anchor's stabilizing field fails, the entire sector collapses and resets, erasing anything—and anyone—left behind.

## The Core Raid Cycle: Step-by-Step Breakdown

Navigating each stage of the **Active Matter time loop** effectively separates seasoned contractors from recruits who perish in the anomaly. The entire gameplay loop functions on a structured five-phase cycle that demands meticulous preparation and decisive in-raid action.

### Phase 1: Base and Workshop Preparation
Your personal base acts as your sanctuary outside the unstable timeline. Here, you manage your stash, equip protective gear, configure weapons, and invest refined resources into workshop upgrades. Upgrading your workbench allows you to craft higher-tier ammunition, medical supplies, and protective suits that withstand anomaly exposure.

### Phase 2: Quantum Infiltration
You can deploy into the unstable zone as a solo operative or in a coordinated fireteam. Upon deployment, the time anchor initiates its containment countdown. Initial minutes are best spent assessing your spawn point, identifying high-tier extraction vectors, and listening for early-game firefights.

### Phase 3: Scavenging, Combat, and Objective Completion
Once on the ground, your primary objective is to gather active matter canisters, rare military components, and research data. This phase is intense PvPvE. You will face rogue synthetic defense platforms, mutated bio-anomalies, and rival operatives competing for the same limited extraction points.

### Phase 4: Timed Extraction Protocol
As the time anchor destabilizes, extraction zones activate. Reaching an extraction point requires holding the perimeter against both environmental hazards and opportunistic players looking to ambush extracting squads. Failing to board or trigger the extraction before the zone collapses results in total gear loss.

### Phase 5: Workshop Progression and Meta Loop
Returning to base with your haul allows you to refine active matter, unlock new crafting blueprints, and trade with corporate liaisons. This meta-progression ensures that even minor tactical successes build long-term power across timeline iterations.

| Phase Name | Primary Objectives | Dominant Threats | Recommended Time Share |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Base Staging** | Mod weapons, manage stash space, select keys | Poor inventory allocation, lack of meds | Pre-Raid (3–5 Mins) |
| **2. Zone Ingress** | Secure spawn perimeter, map extraction routes | Spawn rushers, aggressive AI patrols | First 10% of Raid |
| **3. Resource Raid** | Harvest active matter, loot labs, complete contracts | Rival operatives, heavy combat droids | Middle 60% of Raid |
| **4. Exfiltration** | Reach extraction pad, defend beacon countdown | Exit campers, timeline collapse timer | Final 30% of Raid |
| **5. Post-Raid Reinvestment**| Refine matter, craft ammo, repair body armor | Economy mismanagement | Post-Raid (2–4 Mins) |

## Economy, Death Penalties, and Loot Retention Strategies

Because death in the loop resets your tactical deployment, mitigating gear loss within the **Active Matter time loop** is vital for long-term economic stability. While narrative reconstitution brings your operative back to life at your home base, whatever physical items you carried in your backpack during a failed raid remain lost in the collapsed sector.

To build a sustainable armory, players must adopt strict risk-mitigation rules based on stash organization and resource investment.

| Item Category | Status on Successful Extraction | Status on Death in Raid | Best Practice / Preservation Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Active Matter Canisters** | Transferred to base; used for workshop research | Completely lost to the anomaly | Prioritize securing in safe pouches if available |
| **Equipped Weapons & Armor** | Retained with durability reduction | Dropped on death; claimable by killers | Insure through corporate contracts when possible |
| **Medical Supplies & Ammo** | Unused items return to your inventory | Lost permanently upon death | Bring only what you calculate needing for 2 fights |
| **Base Workshop Blueprints** | Permanently unlocked in base tech tree | Retained permanently if inspected/learned | Consume immediately upon finding in a raid |
| **Specialized Lab Keycards** | Usable for future runs if extracted | Lost if carried in standard backpack slots | Keep in secure container slots during exploration |

### Actionable Tips for Managing Stash Value
1. **Tier Your Raids**: Run low-cost loadouts ("budget runs") when your goal is harvesting raw crafting materials, and reserve top-tier armor and customized rifles for dedicated squad-on-squad high-value vault runs.
2. **Prioritize Tech Unlocks**: Spend gathered active matter on workshop production efficiency first. Being able to craft your own high-penetration ammunition lowers your dependency on market vendors.
3. **Never Overstay the Clock**: Greed is the leading cause of death. If you have secured valuable blueprints or rare components, head toward the nearest extraction point with at least four to five minutes remaining on the stabilization timer.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the Active Matter Time Loop

### How does dying in the Active Matter time loop affect my stash?
When you die during a raid, you lose all unbonded weapons, gear, active matter, and loot in your inventory. However, your home base, unlocked workshop recipes, and items safely stored in your permanent stash remain unaffected by the timeline reset.

### Can time anchors be extended during an active raid?
Based on current game mechanics, time anchor timers are fixed per instance. Once the quantum containment countdown begins, operatives must extract before it hits zero; there are no field mechanics that allow players to delay the overall collapse of the sector.

### Are extraction points always the same in every loop?
While extraction zones are located at designated geographic locations across the map, their availability fluctuates between timeline instances. Some extraction gates require specific keycards, power restoration, or single-use activation protocols, meaning you must always prepare backup routes.

### How do active matter anomalies affect ballistics and gunplay?
Dense active matter zones generate localized gravitational shifts. Inside these anomaly fields, bullet drop, projectile velocity, and grenade trajectories can alter significantly, requiring operatives to adjust their aim compared to standard firefights in unaffected areas.
